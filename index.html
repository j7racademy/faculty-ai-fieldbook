Tomorrow’s teaching, ready to use.

Led by Dr Avneesh Khare · NotebookLM

Dr Asha has a 20-minute Phase II MBBS small-group class tomorrow. She needs an engaging activity and a quick way to see what students understood.

You leave withA 20-minute teaching outline, three retrieval questions, and a checked answer key.
3 min method5 min demonstration8 min practice4 min review
  1. Choose one trusted source. Use an approved departmental handout or the WHO hand-hygiene poster for this demonstration. Add it to a new notebook.
  2. Generate a small teaching kit. Paste the prompt below. Replace the topic with one you actually teach.
  3. Play “spot the leap.” Open one citation. Does the source really support the question and the answer? Remove or repair unsupported content.
Teaching mini-kit · copy into your notebook
I am junior clinical faculty in an Indian medical college teaching Phase II MBBS students.
Use only the source(s) I have selected in this notebook. My topic is hand hygiene. Replace this with my actual topic when I supply different sources.
I have 20 minutes and a small group with mixed confidence levels. No special equipment is available.
Create: (1) one observable learning outcome, (2) a minute-by-minute 20-minute outline with a brief student activity, and (3) three short retrieval questions with an answer key.
Cite the source passage supporting each answer. Mark anything unsupported as “not established in the supplied source”. Do not invent competency codes, facts, references, or clinical recommendations.
End with a short faculty review checklist. Keep the output to one usable page.
Before using it: check all three answers against the source, confirm the level fits your students, and use the competency wording applicable to your own batch and department. Retrieval questions support learning; they are not a validated examination.

Make the message fit your hospital.

Joint session · Jaideep + Avneesh · Gemini

The committee update is due. Dr Asha needs to request missing information from busy colleagues without sounding vague, demanding, or like a chatbot.

You leave withA concise email and a short WhatsApp reminder, with one clear ask and deadline.
3 min method5 min demonstration8 min practice4 min review
  1. Start with your intent. Write three raw bullets: what is needed, by when, and what you can genuinely offer.
  2. Add five bits of context. Role, audience, task, constraints, and output standard. Use the builder below if you prefer.
  3. Play “would I actually send this?” Compare the generic version with your revised draft. Remove invented promises and unnecessary jargon.

Research: find the next honest step.

Led by Dr Jaideep J Rayapudi · Gemini + a short ChatGPT add-on

“I need to do research for promotion” is too broad to act on after rounds. Dr Asha needs a feasible question and a next step she can take with her mentor.

You leave withA research-question shortlist, a feasibility check, and a 25-minute next action.
3 min method5 min Gemini demo6 min practice2 min ChatGPT add-on4 min review
  1. Describe a problem, not a desired publication. Use a fictional teaching question: “Can a brief retrieval activity help students recall material at the next class?”
  2. State your constraints. Teaching access, time, potential supervision, what is unknown, and what approvals must be checked.
  3. Do a pre-mortem. Ask why this project might stall, what evidence is missing, and the smallest mentor discussion you should prepare.
Feasible research next step · copy into Gemini
Act as a critical research-planning partner, not as a substitute for my mentor or ethics committee.
I am junior clinical faculty in an Indian medical college. For this fictional exercise, my problem is passive student participation in short teaching sessions. I am considering a brief retrieval activity and recall at the next class. I have 25 minutes this week to prepare a mentor discussion. Teaching access, supervision, approvals, and outcome measures are still unresolved. Adapt these details to my actual context when provided. Do not assume permission to use patient or student data.
Suggest three focused research questions. For each, list a possible approach, information needed to assess feasibility, likely bias, and the approval or supervision questions I must resolve.
Do not invent data, novelty claims, references, sample-size calculations, or promotion eligibility. State what needs a real literature search or expert review.
Now assume the project stalls after three months. List three plausible reasons and a practical prevention step for each.
End with one 25-minute action that prepares a useful conversation with my mentor this week.

  2. Run a tiny test. Give it a new fictional task. Does it ask for missing information and respect your constraints?

Faculty work buddy · save this instruction
You help me prepare and review professional work as junior clinical faculty in an Indian medical college.
Start by asking which workflow I need: teaching preparation, weekly planning, an administrative message, or research planning. Ask for missing information that materially affects the result.
Use the actual audience, time, resources, source material, and institutional context I provide. Offer the smallest useful version of the task. Keep the output concise and editable.
Do not invent facts, references, competency codes, roster authority, approvals, or available time. If the source does not establish a claim, say so. Do not request identifiable patient material.
Keep patient-care decisions, authorship responsibility, research approvals, and final release decisions with the appropriate human professionals.
End every output with: what I must check, my next action, and a realistic time box. Ask me after use what worked and what should change.
Your seven-day test: use the workflow at least twice. Note actual preparation time and whether the output passed your review. Keep, revise, or stop it based on those observations.

Google · Notebook frequently asked questions ↗
Do I need a paid AI subscription?
The exercises are designed around basic text interactions and selected-source work, with a saved-prompt fallback. Product access and limits vary by account and institution. If a feature is unavailable, follow the live example and use the supplied text material later.
Can I use these prompts in my specialty?
Yes. Change the audience, task, approved sources, resources, and output standard. Keep your specialty unnamed in shared practice material if needed. For teaching, review every answer before students see it.
What should I do when the AI is confidently wrong?
Stop at the unsupported claim. Open the original source, identify the mismatch, and correct or discard the output. Asking a second model can expose a disagreement, but it cannot establish the truth on its own.
Can I use patient records or unpublished institutional data?
Use the fictional practice pack during this workshop. For real work, follow your institution’s approved tools, permissions, confidentiality rules, and professional responsibilities. Do not paste identifiable records into this fieldbook or an unapproved AI account.
Where are my notes saved?
In local browser storage on this device. This page has no submission form, analytics, or account system. Tool links open external services with their own terms. Use “Download my plan” to keep a separate copy.
